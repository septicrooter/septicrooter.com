WHAT NOT TO PUT
IN A SEPTIC SYSTEM

A septic system is a living treatment system. The easiest way to avoid expensive trouble is to stop sending it things it was never designed to handle.

(678) 744-7878
The Rule

IF IT IS NOT WASTEWATER OR SEPTIC-SAFE TOILET PAPER, THINK TWICE

Your septic tank separates solids, grease, and liquid. Bacteria break down some organic material, while clarified liquid moves to the drain field for soil treatment.

Anything that will not break down, anything that adds heavy grease or solids, and anything that disrupts the biology of the tank can shorten the system's life.

The drain field is the expensive part. Good disposal habits are mostly about keeping solids, grease, and harsh materials from reaching it.

Never Flush These

THE FASTEST WAY TO CREATE A BACKUP

Wipes

Even wipes labeled flushable do not behave like toilet paper in septic systems.

Paper towels and tissues

They are built to stay strong when wet, which is exactly the wrong trait in a septic tank.

Feminine products

Tampons, pads, wrappers, and applicators belong in the trash, not the toilet.

Diapers and cotton products

Cotton swabs, cotton balls, dental floss, and diapers can tangle, block, and accumulate.

Cat litter

It adds solids and can create hard, heavy material the system was not designed to process.

Medication

Do not use your drains as a disposal route for medicines. Use a proper take-back option when available.

Kitchen Habits

GREASE AND FOOD SCRAPS ARE FIELD PROBLEMS IN SLOW MOTION

Grease, fats, and oils may go down the sink as liquid, but they cool, float, coat pipes, and add load to the tank.

Food scraps add solids. A garbage disposal can make that worse by sending ground-up food into a system designed for wastewater, not kitchen waste.

Scrape plates into the trash, wipe grease from pans before washing, and avoid using the sink as a disposal system.

Chemicals

WHAT CAN DISRUPT THE SYSTEM

Normal household cleaning, used reasonably, is different from dumping harsh chemicals into the drain.

  • Do not pour paint, solvents, gasoline, pesticides, or automotive fluids into drains.
  • Avoid heavy bleach use or repeated chemical drain cleaners.
  • Do not treat the septic system like a place to dispose of leftover products.
  • If you are relying on additives or chemical fixes, read are septic additives safe? before spending more.
Water Habits

TOO MUCH CLEAN WATER CAN STILL CAUSE TROUBLE

A septic system needs time to separate and treat wastewater. Large bursts of water can push solids through the tank and overload the field.

Spread laundry through the week, repair running toilets, fix dripping fixtures, and keep water softener or treatment equipment from dumping unnecessary volume into the septic system.

If multiple drains are slow or backing up, start with why drains back up and backups after pumping.

Warning Signs

WHEN BAD HABITS MAY ALREADY BE SHOWING UP

Slow drains in more than one area

That often means the issue is downstream of a single fixture.

Gurgling toilets or tubs

Air movement can signal a restriction or overloaded system.

Sewage odor

Odor indoors or outdoors is not normal.

Wet or unusually green field area

The drain field may be receiving more than it can accept.

Good To Know

COMMON QUESTIONS

Are flushable wipes safe for septic systems?
No. Treat wipes as trash, even when the package says flushable.
Can I use a garbage disposal with septic?
Many homes do, but it increases solids in the tank. If you use one, be conservative and keep up with maintenance.
Is bleach safe for septic?
Small household amounts are usually not the issue. Heavy or repeated chemical use is the concern.
Can coffee grounds go down the sink?
No. Coffee grounds add solids and should go in the trash or compost, not the drain.
What toilet paper is best for septic?
Use septic-safe toilet paper that breaks down easily and avoid thick, heavy products when possible.
What should I do if I already flushed something bad?
If it was one small item and nothing is backing up, watch the system. If drains slow, gurgle, or back up, get diagnosis before repeat damage occurs.
