FAILED SEPTIC INSPECTION
DURING A GEORGIA HOME SALE?

Most closings survive septic problems, but only when the diagnosis, estimate, and county timeline get handled quickly.

(678) 744-7878
First Step

FIND OUT WHAT FAILED

Failed is not a standardized term. It may mean a missing riser lid, a tank that could not be fully evaluated, a bad pump, a blocked line, or a drain field that is not accepting effluent.

Ask for the written report, not a phone summary. If the report says the system could not be fully evaluated, that is different from a documented failure.

For the broader negotiation question, see our guide on who pays for septic during a home sale.

Georgia Transfer Reality

CONFIRM THE COUNTY REQUIREMENT EARLY

County environmental health requirements vary. Some transfers need septic documentation, some repairs need permits, and turnaround times change by county and season.

Do not rely on what happened in a neighboring county. Call the environmental health office for the county where the property sits and build that timeline into the closing plan.

Contract Clock

THE SCHEDULE IS OFTEN THE REAL PROBLEM

  • Get the written inspection report.
  • Get an independent diagnosis and written estimate quickly.
  • Confirm county documentation and permitting requirements.
  • Negotiate with actual numbers instead of worst-case guesses.

Resolution Paths

HOW THESE USUALLY GET SOLVED

Seller repairs before closing

Cleanest when there is enough time for repair, inspection, and documentation.

Price adjustment or credit

Works for defined problems with firm estimates, subject to lender and contract limits.

Escrow holdback

Useful when the repair is agreed but weather, permits, or scheduling push work past closing.

Renegotiate or terminate

Usually driven by uncertainty or a serious field failure that the parties cannot absorb.

Buyer And Seller Notes

WHAT TO WATCH FOR

Buyers should ask whether the inspection evaluated the drain field or only the tank, whether the tank was pumped, and whether county records match the house as it stands now.

Sellers can avoid the worst timing by getting a pre-listing septic evaluation, especially when the system is older, has had alarms or backups, or has unclear maintenance history.

If the failure involves a field diagnosis, also read failing field or clogged line.

Good To Know

COMMON QUESTIONS

Does a failed septic inspection kill a home sale in Georgia?
Usually not. Most are resolved through repair, price adjustment, escrow, or renegotiation.
Who pays for septic repairs during a sale?
It is negotiable and depends on the contract, local practice, and the facts. There is no automatic universal rule.
Can I sell a house with a failed septic system?
It may be possible, but it can limit buyers, lending, and transfer documentation. Confirm the county requirement before assuming.
How long does an under-contract septic repair take?
Simple component repairs can be quick. Drain field work may involve soil evaluation, permitting, excavation, and re-inspection.
Does a septic inspection include the drain field?
Not always. Ask directly. A tank-only inspection cannot answer the most expensive question.
